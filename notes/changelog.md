# Changelog

What landed on main, newest first. Only changes you can see or use are listed;
regenerated code, CI, notes and tooling are left out.

## 2026-11-15

- Ray tracing: the boulder in Mayahem Temple casts a whole shadow again. A see-through layer of the floor round it, lying just above the ground, was taken as solid by the traced rays, and cast dark spikes round a lit hole in the boulder's shadow; it is now left out of the traced scene.
- Graphics > Lighting: Keep Game Look, on by default. With Relighting or Ray Tracing the picture keeps the game's own colours and its overall brightness, and the new lighting only moves the light and shade. The brightness is matched from frame to frame, so a new view settles within about half a second. Off, the new lighting sets colours and brightness as before.
- Relighting and Traced Relighting: no more halo of copies round characters and objects. The light bounced off the surroundings, and the ground's colour that lights what faces down, were read from points set round each pixel, so a model standing there was stamped in a ring round itself (a hexagon of halos) and the wall behind it showed through it. They now come from the same points of the view for every pixel. Relighting without ray tracing also no longer glows round Banjo: its guess of the game's shading took in a character standing in front of the ground when the camera looked across it.
- Graphics > Lighting: the settings of the choice made (Relighting's, or all of Ray Tracing's) sit under it, indented, the way Fur's coats do, and Lighting opens and shuts them with its arrow.
- Esc menu: the Sound tab is gone; Main Volume, Music Player and Mix Rate are on General now, under a Sound heading after the controls and the language, before Saves. Your sound settings stay as they were.
- Esc menu: the tab portraits follow your texture packs. Each one is found by the same texture hash the game's own drawing of it gets, so a pack that replaces the game's talking-head portraits (BT Reloaded does, in HD) shows its pictures on the tabs too, and switching a pack on or off changes them at once. Console: `portraits hashes` lists the hashes.
- Voxel Layer: moved from Gameplay to the WIP tab, under its own heading; your settings come with it. The Bill Drill bores down as it drills: Banjo sinks into the ground as it breaks, instead of the hole showing only when the drill ends. The inside of a hole gets darker the deeper it goes, so a hole in a wall reads as a hole and not as a patch on the wall. A hole dug into a floor shows dirt in the floor's colour, no longer a stretched copy of the floor's texture (a dirt path's zigzag showed as flat beige patches).
- Voxel Layer: no more slivers round the holes. Tan strips under a hole in a wall (the ground behind the cliff showing through), thin green strips of the wall across a hole, and spikes poking out of the wall beside one are gone, and `voxel voidscan` finds no gaps. The lip thrown up round a hole dug down is a low, round, uneven mound instead of a box. Cracks in struck stone are a soft darkening of the stone, no longer black lines on a white patch.

## 2026-11-14

- 3D grass: flowers grow in beds now, with plain lawn between, instead of being sprinkled over every lawn: about a tenth of the grass is a flower bed, fullest in its middle and thinning out at its edge, with only a lone flower now and then elsewhere, so there are about half as many flowers at the same Flower Amount. Two new sliders under Graphics > 3D Grass set the beds: Flower Beds (0 to 300%, 100 by default) sets how much of the grass is beds, and Bed Size (25 to 400%, 100 by default) makes them many small ones or a few big fields of flowers. Witchyworld's balloon flowers are round balloons with a shine and a knot, instead of diamonds that looked square.
- 3D grass: small creatures live in it now (Graphics > 3D Grass: Land Creatures and Sea Creatures, both on, and Creature Amount, 100% by default, all applied at once; they need 3D Grass on). They are chunky and flat-coloured like the game's own little creatures, built in the shader rather than taken from the game, with round, twelve-sided bodies shaded smoothly as the N64 shades its own creatures (no facets), and wings, legs and flippers that bend at the middle; frogs, the sea turtle and fish have round eyes with black pupils: round black-and-yellow bees with little wings and big white eyes, butterflies, long dragonflies, plump moths, glowing fireflies, domed beetles (ladybirds in the meadows) and small flies. Each level has its own: bees, butterflies and ladybirds in the meadows (Jinjo Village, Spiral Mountain, the Isle o' Hags), dragonflies, big blue butterflies and green beetles in Terrydactyland, flies, dusty beetles and the odd moth in Glitter Gulch Mine, fireflies and pale moths in Witchyworld's night, flies and grey moths in Grunty Industries, ember fireflies and fiery moths on Hailfire Peaks' lava side, frosty moths and frost-white butterflies on its icy side, bright butterflies, red dragonflies and bees round Mayahem Temple, bees and pastel butterflies in Cloud Cuckooland, and white butterflies, flies and dragonflies by Jolly Roger's Lagoon. With Day and Night on, fireflies and moths come out from dusk to dawn. They rest on the grass's tips (or, where a level has little grass, on its flat ground) and fly from spot to spot. When Banjo comes near they scatter up and away and settle somewhere else, coming back once he has gone, and a bee may buzz round his head for a moment first. Under the sea (Atlantis and the Sea Bottom), small schools of bright, chunky fish (orange, yellow, blue, red, green or purple, with fins and a band of a second colour) and the odd big one on its own cruise over the seabed weed, tails swishing, and dart away, spreading out, when Banjo swims at them. They keep to a ring round Banjo and grow in from nothing and shrink away as they come and go, so none pops in or out. No two land on the same spot or on top of each other, and each has its own temperament (a little faster or slower, warier or bolder, resting longer or shorter), so a crowd of one kind doesn't move as one. New ones appear well away from the camera, never right in front of it, they land and rest away from it too, one that wanders up close to the camera shrinks away before it can fill the screen, and one fleeing towards the camera veers off to the side. Fireflies have a big round tail that glows at full brightness whatever the light, pulsing and now and then flashing but never going out, and stay a few pixels across far off, so they show as points of light in the dark. At most 96 at once, all in one draw, and any behind the camera or off the screen are skipped before their shapes are worked out. On an RTX 5080, 76 of them in Jinjo Village measured within the noise of the GPU's frame time (2.62 ms with them, 2.66 ms with Insects off); their CPU work is 0.002 to 0.006 ms a frame, and the spots they land on take a megabyte or two at most. Besides the insects: birds peck on the ground and wheel high overhead, flying off when Banjo comes near (bluebirds and robins in the meadows and round Mayahem Temple, seagulls by Jolly Roger's Lagoon, crows in Grunty Industries and Glitter Gulch Mine; none by night), frogs sit on the paths with their eyes up on top and hop away from him a few hops at a time (green, a brown toad, a red tree frog), and crabs snap their claws and scuttle sideways on Jolly Roger's Lagoon's shore and town and on the seabed. Under the sea a sea turtle rows slowly past breathing out bubbles and a ray glides by with its wings rippling, both swimming off when he comes close, and the fish let bubbles go that wobble up and pop. Banjo's attacks (the roll, the claw swipe and Kazooie's Wing Whack) send anything near him tumbling away, spinning, and scatter a school of fish wide. The light ones drift with the 3D grass's wind, and a resting butterfly's wings flutter faster in the gusts. Bees can live round a hive too: stand at one and use the console's `critters hive add` (kept in critter_hives.txt next to the game), and a few bees buzz round its mouth and fly out to the grass and back while Banjo is near; none come placed yet. Console: `critters`, `critters list`, `critters spawn [kind] [n]`, `critters reset`, `critters hive add|remove|list|bees`.
- Ray tracing shows more: traced sun shadows and the shadows of models now take away up to 80% of the light instead of 55%, so they read as shade instead of a light grey film. Shadow-map and screen-space shadows are unchanged.
- Ray tracing: Traced Relighting no longer makes the levels brighter and the grass a glaring green. The traced sun is stronger against the sky and the picture is exposed so flat ground in full sun is a little brighter than the game paints it: about the game's brightness on the whole, with more light and shade.
- Ray tracing: lamps light up caves and rooms. Their traced light reaches two and a half times as far as the game's own lamplight and lights the surface's own colour, so a lantern on a cave wall lights the floor and the steps round it.
- Ray tracing: Traced Relighting works in rooms and caves too, relit by the lamps with their shadows where their light reaches; elsewhere they keep the game's picture. Strong lamplight rolls off softly into a warm glow.

## 2026-11-13

- Gameplay > Voxel Layer (WIP, off by default): Banjo digs into the levels' ground and walls with his attacks and the special eggs. The level looks and plays as it always did until he strikes it; behind its surface is 1 to 2 m of ground (2 to 4 m in sand and dirt). Sand and dirt break at one blow, anything else cracks first; nothing near water or metal breaks. The holes are smooth, carved shapes in the texture of the surface he broke, with stone seams; digging down into soft ground throws up a lip round the hole, and pieces cut loose fall as stones. He can stand in the holes and walk into them. Leaving the level, or falling out of bounds through a hole, puts everything back. Voxel Strength sets how much each blow breaks, and Voxel Depth how deep the ground goes for sand and dirt, stone, and rock. Console: `voxel` (`voxel void on` shows any gap into the void in magenta, `voxel voidscan` looks for them).
- Photo mode: Hide Banjo hides his shadow too.
- Photo mode: a Face row holds Banjo's eyes open, half shut or shut (Game leaves them as the game has them), on Banjo himself and mirrored. Kazooie's eyes stay as they are. Console: `photo face [game|open|half|shut]`.
- Photo mode: a Mirror row flips whoever you photograph left to right, Banjo himself or another character, in any pose. Console: `photo mirror [on|off]`.
- Photo mode's pose grid: scrub a card through its loop with C-left and C-right (or by moving the mouse sideways); the card holds that frame with a bar showing where it is, the character in the world holds it too, and A keeps it as the Pose frame.
- Photo mode looks more like the game's own menus: the list's heading has a Jiggy and a honeycomb over a gold rule, the list slides in from the left, the picked row nudges across with its Jiggy popping and its value pops when it changes, and the grid grows open with its pages sliding across as they turn. A viewfinder sits over the picture while photo mode is on: corner brackets, a small cross in the middle, and a blinking red light with a film counter of the photos taken. Like the list, none of it is ever in a photo.
- Graphics settings set after Lighting no longer go back to their defaults when the game starts with Lighting on Ray Tracing. Loading the file set the Ray Tracing switch to match Lighting before the switch was read, which saved the file half loaded.
- Graphics > Retro: CRT is a proper CRT now. It used to darken every other band of the finished picture and lay coloured stripes over it; it now draws the picture again from the game's own 240 lines, each a beam of light that spreads wider where the picture is brighter, so dark areas show black gaps between the lines and bright ones fill in, with the picture as bright overall as without it. The phosphors in front have a choice of CRT Mask (aperture grille, slot mask, shadow mask or none) and the glass can bulge with CRT Curvature. 16-bit colour and the colourblind filter are applied to each of the game's pixels it reads.

## 2026-11-12

- 3D grass is back, remade to be cheap (Graphics > 3D Grass, after Tessellation, off by default; it was on the WIP tab): tufts of broad blades grow on the grassy ground in the ground's own colours, sway in the wind and part round Banjo, all the way across a level (Distance 20000 by default: far off the blades are fewer and broader, so the cost stays small). Near blades are folded along a ridge so they look rounded, clumps vary a little in colour, and gusts of wind brighten the blades they bend. A newly entered area's grass is all there from its first frame, grass grows on slopes as fully as on flat ground, tracks and paths laid over the grass stay clear, and climbing vines are left alone. Roads and paths stay clear. Replace Long Grass (on) covers the flat painted strip of grass on the sides of the raised lawns in front of Jiggywiggy's Temple with real blades from foot to top. Each patch's blades are worked out once and kept, so a frame only picks what to draw. On an RTX 5080 it measured within the noise (under 0.05 ms) at the defaults and 0.13 ms at the highest density, against 0.8 to 1.0 ms for the old 3D grass at its default 1800 reach and 2.05 ms at 6000, in the same spot. Flowers (on) adds little five-petalled flowers, some bigger than others, in patches of the game's bright colours, out as far as the grass; Flower Amount sets how many. Grass Type picks grass, clover, meadow (grass with clover patches) or wild grass. Long Grass Height sets the long grass's height. Blades fade in and out one at a time with their own distance as you move, and grass by a path's edge is shorter. Settings: Density, Distance, Height, Replace Long Grass and Flowers. Console: `grass`, `grass rebake`.
- 3D grass: Banjo treads the grass down. Where he walks the blades lie flat and a little darker and stay down for about ten seconds, then rise again slowly over about a minute and a half, each blade at its own pace, so he leaves a narrow, soft trodden path through the grass that grows back gradually instead of springing up right behind him. Trodden grass lies the way he walked in the middle of his path and is pushed out to the sides at its edges, grass round him leans out of his way as he goes through it, and grass he runs past ripples. Everyone treads the grass now, not only Banjo: the Jinjos, enemies, Mumbo's transformations and anything thrown or falling flatten it where they touch the ground, and a hard landing (a ground pound, a long fall) flattens a ring of grass round him, wider the harder he lands. Grass by a path's edge thins out and shortens over three steps, with fewer, thinner blades, instead of standing over the path. The far edge of the grass melts into the ground's own colour instead of ending in a line. Each level has its own plants, in their own shapes and colours, with three grasses and three flowers of its own, and its usual grass is itself a mix of four, fine tall blades, broad short ones, long ones curling over and plain ones, each a shade apart, so a field no longer looks like one blade copied: the Isle o' Hags, Jinjo Village and Spiral Mountain grow timothy with pale seed spikes, broad dark sedge and blue fescue among the grass, and daisies, nodding bluebells and buttercups besides the usual flowers; At 100 they are tuned to go together: the quiet grasses (sedge, tussocks, frozen tufts, ash grass, wire grass, rushes) about one blade in ten, the bold, tall or glowing ones (cattails, bamboo, palm fronds, neon grass, obsidian spikes, rainbow grass) a few in a hundred as accents, and the delicate flowers most of a level's flowers with the showy ones now and then. Ground that comes into view near the camera gets its grass the frame it appears instead of growing it in, and grass already worked out is no longer thrown away and redone in the middle of play. 3D grass is about ten times cheaper on the GPU (0.19 ms to 0.02 ms in Jinjo Village on an RTX 5080): it drew every flower it could show at the highest Flower Amount and left most of them out corner by corner, even with Flowers off; it now draws only the flowers Flower Amount shows. Its blades also take half the memory: each is kept in 16 bytes instead of 32 (about 125 MB less in RAM and on the GPU in Jinjo Village), and the grass no longer keeps its own copy of them besides the renderer's (about 50 MB less RAM in Wooded Hollow), with no change to how the grass looks. Entering a map no longer stops the game while all its grass is worked out (about 215 ms in Jinjo Village): the grass is worked out on its own thread, worked out on several cores at once, and the game waits only for the grass near and in view (about 30 ms in Jinjo Village, 15 in Wooded Hollow), so the grass in sight is still there from the first frame. Working the grass out also takes about 40% less time than before, with exactly the same blades. The blades are kept on the GPU only, sent up through a buffer of their own size that is let go of after (about 120 MB less RAM in Wooded Hollow), and the renderer no longer takes a lock or goes through a long grass strip's triangles for every draw every frame. Blades no longer dip into hillsides and come up out of them again as the wind or Banjo bends them (it looked like grass growing in on slopes): each keeps the slope it stands on and its tip stays above the ground. Replace Long Grass now also covers the strips of long grass running up a slope beside a path and the banks that lean back a little (in Wooded Hollow, by the temple and where Banjo arrives), which were left flat. Seen towards the sun, the tips of sunlit grass glow a warm yellow-green, as grass does against the light (not in shade or caves). 3D grass is also about half as costly on the GPU (0.46 to 0.27 ms in Mayahem Temple and 0.49 to 0.25 ms in Wooded Hollow on an RTX 5080, with no change to how it looks): blades are coloured by a small shader of their own instead of the one built for everything the game draws, only the plants that need the finer shape (ferns, palm fronds, reeds, bamboo, seaweeds) are drawn with it in levels that have them, the rest with the simple seven-cornered one, and a blade no longer works out a flower's shape it does not use.
